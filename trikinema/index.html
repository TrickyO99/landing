Trikinemarikinema.

I wanted my films and my music on the big screen, my way. So I built the pieces: a small server on my PC, an app on the TV, one on my phone.

FilmsNow Playing

Project one

Films

My own Plex. The films sit in a folder on my PC. The TV shows them as a proper menu: posters, synopsis, cast, ratings. Press OK and it plays.

  • A real menu. Home with shelves (continue watching, recently added, top rated, one per genre), a Browse grid with sorting and filters, and a film page for each title.
  • Search that finds things. Titles, cast, directors, genres, years. Type on the screen keyboard, or on a real one.
  • Subtitles on their own. It finds English and French subtitles for every film and times them to the audio, so they don’t drift.
  • Linked to Letterboxd. My rating shows next to the crowd’s, films I’ve seen get a badge, and my watchlist becomes a shelf. Read-only: Letterboxd has no open way to write back, so finished films come out as a file I can import.
  • Picks up where I stopped, on the TV or the phone.
  • Python
  • Android TV
  • Java
  • Media3 / ExoPlayer
  • TMDB
  • ffmpeg
  • Letterboxd

How the pieces fit

  1. The server

    A Python program with no dependencies. It scans the folder, reads each file’s codecs, fetches the film details from TMDB, and streams the file with seeking.

  2. The TV app

    Finds the server by itself on the Wi-Fi, no address to type. Built on Android’s Media3 player.

  3. The phone app

    Same library, same code, laid out for a phone. It plays the film on the phone.

  4. The subtitles

    Downloaded once, timed against the sound of the film, then kept. Nothing to do on movie night.

Good to know. Everything stays at home: the server only answers devices on my own network, and the only things that leave are film titles and ids, sent to TMDB and the subtitle site, plus a request for my public Letterboxd feed. The PC has to be on to watch.

Project two

Now Playing

Google TV has no screen for what’s playing on Apple Music. So I made one: the cover, the song, what’s next, and the lyrics scrolling in time, in the spirit of Apple’s own TV app.

  • Only Apple Music. The phone app listens to Apple Music’s playback and nothing else. No other app, no microphone.
  • Live lyrics. The current line is bright, the rest fade out. A Sing-along mode puts them centre stage.
  • The remote works both ways. Arrows on the TV remote skip, pause or go back, and the phone obeys.
  • It pops up by itself when music starts, and closes shortly after it stops.

The lyrics come from a free lyrics database. Only the song, artist and album names are sent, and there’s a switch to turn that off.

  • Android
  • Java
  • MediaSession
  • mDNS
  • lrclib

What it can’t do. Apple’s karaoke slider, the one that turns the singer down, uses Apple’s own audio separation. I can’t reach that, so Sing-along shows the lyrics only.
